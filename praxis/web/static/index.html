The thesis

Most decision assumptions are estimates in disguise.

A decision says “this will take six weeks”. That is not a fact about the world — it is a forecast, made by a person whose forecasts have a track record. Praxis stores both halves in one graph, so the track record can reach back and invalidate the decision that leaned on it.

The statistics are arithmetic, never a model. Below five samples the calibrator refuses to speak rather than guessing, and it says so.

01 Capture. Decisions, the assumptions under them, and the evidence each one cites.
02 Compile. Assumptions become predicates with expiry conditions a machine can check.
03 Predict. Every estimate is logged before the work, never after it.
04 Calibrate. Bias is measured per person, per class of work — or refused.
05 Fuse. Calibration re-prices the assumption, and the decision flips or holds.
06 Argue. A challenger prosecutes each finding before a person ever sees it.
